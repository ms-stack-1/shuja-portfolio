An operations layer instead of an org layer

I started with discovery: mapping the manual workflows with ownership and management, current state to future state, and ranking automation candidates by the hours and cost each would recover. The platform that came out of it runs on the Claude API, Zapier and Make, Node.js and SQL, fronted by Slack, where 14 non-technical staff trigger and manage workflows in plain language. The point-of-sale system, Modisoft, has no API, and browser-agent navigation proved fragile to UI changes, so shift cashiers export daily CSV files into named Google Drive folders that the Make workflows pick up.

The workflows cover reconciliation, reporting, scheduling, purchasing support and administrative coordination, and together they enabled about $105K in annualized wage reductions. Claude-assisted reconciliation matches 6 bank accounts against Modisoft deposits, purchases, expenses and card settlements; it cleared a 9-month backlog in 1 week, work that used to take an assistant 20 hours a week. Daily reporting went from 2 hours per site to 15 minutes across all 3, with cash variance auto-flagged to the responsible employee, and multi-location KPI tracking from about 6 hours a day to 45 minutes. A person checks every flagged reconciliation entry and every suggested price before anything changes, and I still spend about 5 hours a week checking and correcting the suite.

Purchasing and pricing ride on top. Since May 2026, sales-based replenishment compares live stock with trailing 7-, 15- and 30-day sales for about 150 items per store, and Slack delivers recommended quantities every Tuesday. Since June 2026, purchase-price monitoring flags supplier cost changes and proposes retail repricing for approval. With those controls, the inside-store spread between sales and supplier purchases went from about 30% to 45%, sustained since July 2026 (a spread, not gross margin). Slack-based kitchen preparation schedules cut Store 1's discarded food from about $100 to $10 a day at retail value.

The marketing still pays rent. I launched Meta and Google advertising from scratch across the 3 locations at about $20K a month in actual spend; from August to September 2026, at the same $12,000 Meta spend, cost per recorded outcome fell from $28.57 to $20.00. A relaunched in-house food brand, with redesigned menus, signage and promotion, lifted daily revenue 87%, from about $800 to $1,500, and brand compliance audit scores rose from 60-65% to 99% across all 3 locations.

Why this matters for a marketing ops / analyst seat

Strip the retail context away and the role is a marketing operations job description: own the systems (automation platform, data pipeline, reporting layer), guarantee data integrity (reconciliation against source records, a person reviewing every flagged entry), run the analytics (SQL on 60K+ transactions monthly, sales-based replenishment), and translate it for ownership (Power BI dashboards and reporting). The difference is that here, a broken workflow doesn't dent a dashboard; it holds up the books. Doing that alone, reporting to ownership, is the strongest evidence I have that I can be trusted with a revenue team's operational backbone.
