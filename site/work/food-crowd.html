Trust the purchase data first, then scale it

The first problem was measurement. Food Crowd's inherited accounts reported purchases that didn't match the store's orders, so I audited purchase tracking, rebuilt the conversion configuration, prioritized completed Purchase events over product views, cart adds and checkout starts, and compared every platform's purchases with backend orders. In the combined reporting view, each order was assigned to one paid channel, so Google and Meta could no longer both claim the same basket.

Then the content and the funnel. I rebuilt the acquisition stages around purchase-focused product photography, short video, promotions, influencer-box content and retargeting, and sent ad traffic to the matching product collection instead of a generic page. On product landing pages, sessions with an add-to-cart rose from 12.0% to 17.0%. On Google, at an unchanged $3,200 a month, search-term exclusions, product segmentation and budget reallocation cut cost per attributed order from $4.51 to $2.67 between May–June and August–September 2021.

One test I chose to lose. A July 2021 landing-page test put "15% off your first grocery order" against standard pricing, with 3,000 visitors per arm. The discount page won on purchase rate (9.0% against 8.0%), but revenue per visitor fell from $4.00 to $3.83, so I kept the value-focused page. Revenue per visitor was the primary metric, set before the test.

full_depth · for readers and machines
Execution detail: paid media
  • Google: category, product and grocery-delivery Search plus Shopping. August–September 2021 monthly averages: $3,200 spend, 8,000 clicks at $0.40, 1,200 attributed orders at $2.67 each.
  • Meta: prospecting and retargeting with purchase-focused creative. August–September 2021 monthly averages: $4,800 spend, 24,000 link clicks at $0.20, 1,808 attributed orders at $2.65 each.
  • Attribution: conversions reconciled against unique backend order IDs, each order assigned to one paid channel; no randomized incrementality, first-time CAC or profit uplift is claimed.
  • Testing: a 14-day minimum, a planned sample of 3,000 visitors per arm, revenue per visitor as the primary metric and contribution as a guardrail, all fixed before launch.
Execution detail: landing pages & SEO
  • Landing pages: ads matched to the relevant product collection, with imagery, pricing and promotions aligned to the ad, simpler mobile navigation and a clearer Add to Cart; add-to-cart sessions rose from 12.0% to 17.0% in a before/after comparison.
  • SEO: first-page Google rankings for competitive keywords within 9 months, a free acquisition channel compounding under the paid program.
